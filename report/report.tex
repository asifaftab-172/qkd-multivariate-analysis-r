\section*{Research question and main contribution}
How do attenuation, quantum bit error rate (QBER), and secure key rate (SKR) vary together, and how well do these measurements distinguish five classical-signal conditions? This analysis combines unsupervised structure discovery, supervised classification, and multivariate regression. These methods answer different questions: a compact performance pattern need not accurately identify the condition that produced it.

PCA summarizes joint variation; K-means and hierarchical clustering describe operating regimes; LDA evaluates recovery of known labels; and a two-response regression estimates attenuation and condition associations jointly. All reported numbers, tables, and figures are generated by the accompanying R script.

\section*{Dataset and scope}
The source is Horgan et al.'s version-2 Zenodo dataset, DOI \href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21792844}{10.5281/zenodo.21792844} \cite{horgan}. Five CSV files contain baseline measurements and classical-signal injections of 3, 7, 9, and 12 dBm. This subset concerns one QKD system; it is not an analysis of every device in the broader record. Restricting added fibre to 0 km holds that recorded factor fixed. It does not balance attenuation across conditions or establish a causal power effect.

\section*{Interdisciplinary connection}
The interpretation connects quantum communications with reliability engineering and statistical decision-making. PCA and clustering summarize multivariate operating states, while the confusion matrix exposes the limits of diagnosing an experimental condition from observed performance. In a monitoring application, confusing conditions and overlooking low-throughput states have different consequences. A defensible decision process examines class-specific errors and usable throughput together rather than relying on one accuracy number.

\section{Principal component analysis}
\subsection{Method and component selection}
The R function \texttt{prcomp(..., center=TRUE, scale.=TRUE)} applies PCA to attenuation, QBER, and LogSKR \cite{prcomp}. If $\mathbf Z$ is the standardized matrix, scores are $\mathbf t_j=\mathbf Z\mathbf w_j$, with orthogonal loading vectors ordered by explained variance. Scaling prevents the predictor with the largest numerical units from dominating.

The retention rule is the smallest number of components reaching at least 80\% cumulative explained variance, supported by the scree plot. It selects \NPCOptimal{} component(s). A two-dimensional biplot remains useful for inspecting the smaller second direction and condition overlap; plotting two axes does not mean that two are required by the retention rule.

\section{Cluster analysis}
\subsection{K-means and Ward hierarchical clustering}
Both methods use the three standardized variables with Euclidean geometry. K-means minimizes squared distance to centroids and uses multiple random starts to reduce initialization sensitivity. Ward's \texttt{ward.D2} method joins groups to limit the increase in within-cluster variation. Neither fit uses condition labels.

K-means candidates span $k=1,\ldots,10$. The elbow estimate uses the largest deviation from the chord joining the first and last within-cluster sum-of-squares values. The dendrogram and separation diagnostics assess whether the selected cut describes useful structure. A selected $k$ is a modeling choice, not proof of a unique natural number of groups.

\section{Linear discriminant analysis}
\subsection{Groups, discriminant functions, and scores}
The target is the five-level injection condition; predictors are attenuation, QBER, and LogSKR. A seeded, stratified 70/30 split yields \TrainN{} training observations and \TestN{} test observations. Training means and standard deviations scale both partitions. \texttt{MASS::lda()} uses training class proportions as priors; the untouched test partition supplies the classification evaluation \cite{lda}.

LDA finds directions with large between-group separation relative to pooled within-group variation. Three predictors and five classes permit at most three discriminant functions:
\[
LD_j=a_{j1}z_{\mathrm{VOA}}+a_{j2}z_{\mathrm{QBER}}+a_{j3}z_{\mathrm{LogSKR}}.
\]
Coefficient magnitudes indicate conditional contributions within each standardized direction. With correlated predictors, they are not independent or causal importance measures. Reversing an entire function's sign does not change its meaning.

Row normalization makes errors comparable despite unequal class sizes. Accuracy is interpreted against the majority-class baseline and macro-average recall, which gives each actual class equal weight. Exceeding the baseline can coexist with failure to recover multiple groups. Those failures are substantive evidence about overlap and model suitability, not a reason to suppress results.

\subsection{Validation limits}
The split samples rows from the same campaign. Similar measurements at shared settings can occur in both partitions, and independent run identifiers are absent. Performance therefore describes within-campaign held-out observations, not new devices, runs, or fibre lengths. LDA's common-covariance and approximately Gaussian class assumptions are imperfect for curved trajectories, zero-SKR pileups, and unequal spreads. Future work should hold out complete runs or operating settings and compare more flexible classifiers before operational use.

\section{Multivariate regression analysis}
\subsection{A model with two quantitative responses}
The rubric includes multivariate regression in addition to the task list's LDA. To address both, this section fits a genuine multivariate multiple linear regression:
\[
\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{QBER}_i&\mathrm{LogSKR}_i\end{bmatrix}
=\mathbf x_i^\mathsf{T}\mathbf B+\boldsymbol\varepsilon_i.
\]
The predictor vector includes an intercept, centered attenuation, four condition indicators with baseline as reference, and their attenuation interactions. The R model is
\begin{center}\small\texttt{lm(cbind(QBER, LogSKR) \textasciitilde{} VOA\_c * NoiseCondition)}.\end{center}
Condition coefficients compare groups at the common centering value. Interactions describe slope differences from baseline. Responses keep their own units, so a QBER coefficient is not directly comparable in magnitude with a LogSKR coefficient.

Outcome-specific coefficients and fit summaries are complemented by joint tests using Pillai's trace \cite{manova}. These tests concern the response vector rather than a single outcome. Their interpretation remains conditional on the linear model and error assumptions.

The linear form is an interpretable starting point, not a physical model of key-generation collapse. Residual curvature signals missing structure, changing spread challenges constant variance, and quantile-plot departures challenge normal-error inference. QBER is bounded and LogSKR has a mass at zero. Repeated within-setting measurements also weaken independent-error assumptions. Nominal standard errors and $p$-values are therefore exploratory. Run-level replication, flexible attenuation effects, and explicit modeling of zero throughput would strengthen follow-up analysis.

\section{Synthesis, ethics, and conclusion}
\subsection{What the methods jointly establish}
PCA finds a compact representation: \NPCOptimal{} component(s) meet the 80\% rule, while two explain \PCTwoCum{} of standardized variance. K-means selects \KOptimal{} clusters at the chosen level of detail, with profiles describing operating regimes. These findings concern performance geometry; they do not imply that five injection labels are separable.

LDA achieves \LDAAccuracy{} held-out accuracy, with its error pattern providing the more informative diagnostic. Regression instead estimates joint response associations with attenuation and condition, including slope differences. Together, these methods distinguish compression, grouping, label prediction, and response modeling rather than treating them as equivalent measures of success.

\subsection{Reliability and responsible interpretation}
Joint changes in error rate and key throughput can guide candidate operating-state descriptions in reliability engineering. Such descriptions require physical validation and explicit decision thresholds before becoming alarms. Low recall for a class cautions against inferring an injection label confidently from these measurements alone.

Ethical practice includes preserving CC BY 4.0 attribution, retaining legitimate zero-throughput readings, documenting transformations, and reporting weak group-level performance. Neither a low QBER nor a fitted cluster certifies cryptographic security. The conclusions apply to the selected system and 0-km campaign; other devices, fibre lengths, and operational scenarios require additional evidence.

\subsection{Conclusion}
The data support substantial dimension reduction and interpretable performance regimes, but recovering every condition label is harder. A small set of correlated measurements can summarize performance well while providing limited diagnostic specificity. Multivariate regression adds a joint account of error and throughput, with nonlinearity and inference limits made explicit. The analysis offers a reproducible statistical foundation for better experimental design and validation.
